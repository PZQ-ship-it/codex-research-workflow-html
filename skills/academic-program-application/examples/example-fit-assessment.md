# Example Fit Assessment

## Applicant Profile Summary

Placeholder applicant: MA student in religious studies with a 3.78 GPA, thesis on Chinese Protestant print culture, strong Chinese and English, no publication, one conference presentation, and a 25-page writing sample based on missionary periodicals.

## Strengths

- Coherent intellectual trajectory from coursework to thesis topic.
- Writing sample seems directly relevant to modern Chinese religious history.
- Language preparation supports primary-source work.
- Conference experience suggests early professional engagement.

## Weaknesses / Risks

- No archival experience is mentioned.
- The proposed project may still be too broad.
- Faculty fit must be verified program by program.
- Funding risk is high if applying as an international student to unfunded MA programs.

## Program Fit

Strong if the department supports modern China, religious history, and transnational history. Weak if the program's East Asia strength is primarily premodern or literature-focused.

## Faculty Fit

Name only faculty whose current work overlaps with the applicant's source base, geography, period, and method.

## Writing Sample Fit

The writing sample should foreground argument, source method, and historiography. It should not read like a descriptive survey.

## Funding and International-Student Considerations

Verify stipend, tuition remission, health insurance, visa eligibility, and language test requirements.

## Reach / Target / Safer Assessment

Likely target-to-reach for strong PhD programs, depending on writing sample quality and advisor availability.

## Recommended Next Steps

- Narrow the project to a sharper research question.
- Revise the writing sample around argument and sources.
- Build a list based on faculty fit before ranking.
- Verify funding guarantees before applying.

## Overall Evaluation

The profile is plausible for PhD applications, but the application will depend heavily on the writing sample, faculty fit, and a precise project statement.
