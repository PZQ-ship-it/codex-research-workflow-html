# Example SOP Draft

My proposed graduate study examines how Chinese Protestant periodicals in the Republican era shaped new forms of religious public discourse. In my MA thesis, I studied a small corpus of church newspapers from the 1920s and 1930s, focusing on how editors translated theological language into debates about education, citizenship, and social reform. That project led me to a broader question: how did Protestant print networks help create a modern religious public in China, and how did Chinese writers adapt imported Christian concepts to local political and social concerns?

My preparation for this project combines coursework in modern Chinese history, religious studies, and historical method. In seminars on Chinese intellectual history and Christianity in Asia, I developed the historiographical framework for studying religion not only as doctrine but also as institution, media practice, and social imagination. My writing sample, based on a close reading of selected periodicals, demonstrates my ability to work with Chinese-language primary sources and to place them in conversation with scholarship on print culture and religious modernity.

At Example University, I hope to work with Professor A, whose research on modern China and transnational religious networks closely relates to my interest in Protestant media. I am also interested in the department's East Asia workshop and the resources of the Center for Religion and Public Life. These resources would help me refine my project from a study of individual periodicals into a dissertation on religious print networks, institutional authority, and public culture.

In the long term, I hope to pursue research and teaching on modern Chinese religion, Christianity in Asia, and the history of global religious exchange. Example University's combination of historical training, faculty expertise, and interdisciplinary religious studies resources would provide the environment I need for this next stage of work.
