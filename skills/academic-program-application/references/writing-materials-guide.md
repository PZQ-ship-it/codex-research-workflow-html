# Writing Materials Guide

Use this for drafting, rewriting, critiquing, and polishing application materials.

## Supported Materials

- Statement of purpose
- Personal statement
- Research proposal or proposed research project
- CV or resume
- Academic biography
- Writing sample framing note
- Funding statement
- Assistantship application essay
- Scholarship application essay
- Diversity statement where required
- Portfolio statement or artist statement where relevant

## Core Writing Process

1. Identify genre, target program, degree type, discipline, prompt, and word limit.
2. Check whether enough real applicant information is available.
3. If enough information is available, produce a draft or revision.
4. Preserve the user's real background, intellectual voice, and academic trajectory.
5. Strengthen logic, specificity, disciplinary fit, faculty/program fit, and evidence.
6. Remove generic admissions cliches.
7. Critique the result from an admissions or faculty-reader perspective.
8. Suggest the next revision.

## Genre Differences

- SOP: intellectual trajectory, research fit, faculty/program fit, preparation, future goals.
- Personal statement: formative experiences, values, obstacles, identity, motivation, and institutional fit without becoming generic.
- Research proposal: research question, literature gap, method, evidence, feasibility, contribution.
- CV: scannable evidence of preparation, publications, presentations, research, teaching, language, methods, and service.
- Funding statement: financial need, merit, project fit, public/academic impact, feasibility.

## Style Rules

- Prefer specific nouns and concrete evidence.
- Show development over time.
- Tie claims to coursework, papers, research, practice, or experience.
- Use discipline-appropriate vocabulary without overclaiming.
- Name faculty and resources only when the connection is precise.
- Make transitions argumentative, not merely chronological.

## Avoid Or Rewrite

- "I have always been passionate about"
- "prestigious university"
- "broaden my horizons"
- "I am a perfect fit"
- "world-class faculty"
- "interdisciplinary environment"
- "Since childhood..."

Replace these with specific evidence, intellectual stakes, and program-level fit.
