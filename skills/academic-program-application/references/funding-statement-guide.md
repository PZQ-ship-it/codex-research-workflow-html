# Funding Statement Guide

Use this when drafting scholarship, fellowship, assistantship, tuition-aid, or financial-need statements.

## Information To Gather

- Funding type and prompt
- Eligibility criteria
- Degree program and school
- Applicant financial constraints
- Academic merit evidence
- Research, teaching, service, or professional contribution
- International student status if relevant
- Other funding sources or gaps

## Structure

1. Applicant and program context
2. Funding need or merit basis
3. Evidence of preparation and contribution
4. How funding enables the degree plan
5. Expected academic, professional, or public impact

## Tone

Be direct, specific, and dignified. Avoid desperation, entitlement, or inflated claims. For need-based statements, connect financial need to access and feasibility. For merit-based statements, connect past evidence to future contribution.

## Assistantship Essays

For teaching assistantships, emphasize teaching experience, communication, mentoring, grading, discussion leadership, language capacity, and inclusive pedagogy. For research assistantships, emphasize methods, reliability, technical skills, domain knowledge, and prior research contribution.

## Warnings

- Do not invent financial hardship.
- Do not claim funding guarantees.
- Do not imply assistantship eligibility without checking official rules.
- Verify whether international students are eligible.
