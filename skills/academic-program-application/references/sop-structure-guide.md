# Statement Of Purpose Structure Guide

Use this for SOP drafting or critique.

## Core Purpose

An SOP should show what the applicant wants to study, why they are prepared, why the program is a strong fit, and what intellectual or professional trajectory the degree supports.

## Recommended Structure

1. Opening research problem or intellectual question
2. Short account of how the applicant arrived at the question
3. Evidence of preparation: coursework, thesis, papers, research, methods, languages, lab/studio/archive work
4. Proposed direction for graduate study
5. Faculty and program fit
6. Career or scholarly goals
7. Closing statement of readiness and fit

## Field Emphasis

- Humanities: conceptual stakes, texts/archives/histories, languages, writing sample fit, faculty conversations.
- Social sciences: research question, method, evidence, data, theory, feasibility.
- STEM: lab fit, technical skills, prior research outputs, methods, advisor alignment.
- Professional academic degrees: institutional fit, vocation, concrete experience, degree necessity.

## Strong SOP Signals

- The applicant sounds like a developing scholar or practitioner.
- Research interests are specific but not prematurely rigid.
- Faculty fit is concrete and non-generic.
- The writing sample supports the stated direction.
- Weaknesses or shifts are explained through growth and evidence.

## Common Problems

- Autobiography overwhelms research purpose.
- Faculty paragraph reads like copied website text.
- Program fit is just prestige language.
- The applicant claims a project that is too large or underprepared.
- The SOP hides a field shift instead of explaining it.
