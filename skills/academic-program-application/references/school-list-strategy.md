# School List Strategy

Use this when the user asks for reach, target, safer, comparable programs, or a balanced application list.

## Inputs Needed

- Degree type and discipline
- Applicant profile summary
- Research interests
- Geographic limits
- Funding constraints
- Citizenship/international status if relevant
- Preferred advisors or subfields
- Career goals
- Existing target programs

## Selection Criteria

Prioritize:

- Faculty fit and advisor availability
- Program structure and degree expectations
- Funding model and financial risk
- Writing sample and methodology fit
- Placement outcomes
- Language, fieldwork, lab, clinical, studio, or professional requirements
- Location constraints where relevant

Use ranking and prestige as secondary context.

## Recommended List Shape

For a typical North American graduate applicant:

- 2-4 reach programs
- 4-6 target programs
- 2-4 safer programs

Adjust for field norms, application cost, funding needs, and selectivity. For PhD applications, fewer but more precise matches may be better than a broad list with weak fit.

## Program Recommendation Template

For each program:

- Program and university
- Category: reach, target, safer, or exploratory
- Why it fits
- Faculty/resources to examine
- Funding considerations
- Main risk
- What to verify on official pages

## Warnings

- Do not guarantee admission.
- Do not recommend a school only because it is prestigious.
- Do not call a program "safe" when funding is uncertain or applicant fit is weak.
- Do not over-rely on rankings when faculty fit is thin.
