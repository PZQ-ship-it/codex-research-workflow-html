# Fit Assessment Rubric

Use this rubric when the user asks whether they are competitive, whether a program fits them, or how strong their application is.

## Evaluation Dimensions

| Dimension | Strong Evidence | Risk Signals |
|---|---|---|
| Academic preparation | Strong grades in relevant courses, advanced coursework, clear field preparation | Weak grades in core courses, missing prerequisites, unclear transcript context |
| Research fit | Specific research questions align with program strengths | Vague interests, mismatch with department methods or fields |
| Faculty fit | Named faculty with concrete intellectual overlap | Only prestige-based names, no current advisor match |
| Writing sample fit | Sample demonstrates discipline, method, evidence, and argument | Sample unrelated to field, too descriptive, weak argument |
| Research experience | Thesis, RA work, publications, conference papers, lab/studio/archive work | No sustained project or unclear role |
| Intellectual trajectory | Coherent movement from past work to proposed study | Abrupt shifts with no explanation |
| Language/method preparation | Required languages, statistics, coding, lab, archival, or field methods | Missing required preparation for proposed work |
| Professional goals | Goals match degree type and program outcomes | Degree not needed for stated goal |
| Funding risk | Funding path realistic | High tuition exposure, uncertain funding, visa/language constraints |

## Reach / Target / Safer Categories

- Reach: applicant has meaningful fit but one or more major risks; program is highly selective; funding or faculty availability is uncertain.
- Target: applicant's preparation and fit are credible relative to program expectations; still no guarantee.
- Safer: applicant appears above typical preparation for the program and fit is clear; funding and admissions remain uncertain.

Do not assign categories as promises. Admissions outcomes are probabilistic.

## Required Output Logic

1. Summarize applicant profile.
2. Identify evidence-backed strengths.
3. Identify risks without hiding them.
4. Assess academic, research, faculty, writing sample, language, and funding fit.
5. Assign reach/target/safer with rationale.
6. Recommend concrete next steps.

## Common Strategic Advice

- Replace vague intellectual interests with two or three precise research questions.
- Connect past coursework or papers to future research.
- Name faculty only when the overlap is specific.
- Make the writing sample support the SOP's field identity.
- Explain disciplinary shifts directly.
- Address weaknesses through evidence, not apology.
