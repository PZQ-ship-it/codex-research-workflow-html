# Personal Statement Guide

Use this when the prompt asks for personal background, motivation, adversity, identity, vocation, service, leadership, diversity, or values.

## Distinction From SOP

The personal statement explains who the applicant is and what experiences shaped their path. It should complement the SOP, not repeat it.

## Productive Content

- Formative experiences connected to academic direction
- Obstacles or constraints that shaped preparation
- Community, professional, ministry, artistic, legal, or public commitments
- Evidence of maturity, resilience, ethical awareness, or leadership
- How the degree fits the applicant's future contribution

## Structure

1. Specific scene, problem, or turning point
2. Reflection on what changed intellectually or ethically
3. Evidence through action, study, work, or service
4. Connection to the target degree and community
5. Future contribution

## Risks

- Trauma dumping without analysis
- Generic resilience language
- Overly private disclosure not connected to the application
- Repeating the SOP
- Claiming moral virtue without evidence

## Revision Principle

Make the statement personal enough to be distinctive and professional enough to serve the application.
